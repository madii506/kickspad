How KICKS settles, and where it can fail.

5 min readeverything here comes from the rule, not from a number

Is this automated? No. A human presses send.

Kick has no API for gifting. There is no endpoint a program can call to buy a pack and send it to a channel. Gifts are bought and sent by a person, in a Kick account, on a screen. So when a pot covers a pack and the channel is live, a human with a Kick account buys the pack and presses send.

That human decides nothing. Which channel is fixed by the coin's description. When is fixed by whether the channel is live. How much is fixed by the pot and Kick's pack sizes. Their role is the one step the rail does not expose to software. Here is what they can and cannot do.

It cannotSo it

Note 1Narrowing this role is the main engineering work ahead. It will not be described as gone before it is gone. If Kick ever exposes gifting to software, the role goes away, and this page will say so on the day it does.

The rule

Launch a coin on any Kick streamer. Fees send Kicks on stream. The launch control is a Kick channel URL; that is the whole form. The coin's creator fee fills a pot for that channel, and whenever the channel is live and the pot covers a gift pack, Kicks are bought and sent on that stream. The gift alert fires on their screen, in front of their chat, with the coin's name on it.

100% of the creator fee, into Kicks
1 channel per coin
0 things the streamer does

pump.fun lets the creator set the creator fee at launch, capped at 3.00%. Whatever it is set to, KICKS routes 100% of it into the pot for that one channel. There is no split on our side, no treasury, no protocol take netted out before the pot sees it.

The send condition is two facts, and both are checkable by anyone watching: the channel is live, and the pot covers a pack. If either is false, nothing is sent.

Pairing a coin with a channel

There is no form on this site and no registry. The coin's own description on pump.fun says which channel it pays. That line is the pairing key:

Fees → kick.com/<channel> via KICKS

One channel per coin was a decision, not a limitation. A coin that pays five streamers is a coin about nobody. A coin that pays one is about that one. If you want to pay a second streamer, launch a second coin.

Note 2The streamer connects nothing, pastes nothing, signs nothing. There is no code for their bio, no login, no claim window. The Kicks land on their stream whether they have heard of the coin or not.

Settlement order

  1. A coin is created on pump.fun with the description line naming one Kick channel. Its creator fee is set there, capped at 3.00%.
  2. Every trade pays the creator fee. 100% of it is the pot for that channel. The pot climbs; it does not drip, because Kicks are sold in fixed packs.
  3. Two facts are checked: the channel is live, and the pot covers a pack. Both are checkable by anyone watching.
  4. When both are true, a human with a Kick account buys the largest pack the pot covers and presses send on that stream.
  5. Kick fires the gift alert on the streamer's screen, in their layout, at a timestamp in their VOD. That alert is the receipt. No amount is ever drawn by this site.
  6. The send is posted publicly with a clip or a screenshot of the alert as it fired, and the pot balance before and after.

The rungs of the ladder are Kick's prices, not ours. They will be published from Kick's own prices at the moment the first pack is bought, and not before. Kick's share of every gift will be published once confirmed from Kick's own terms, with a link to the terms.

When it goes wrong

Every one of these has an answer decided in advance, rather than after somebody notices.

CaseWhat happensWhere you see it
The channel never goes liveIts Kicks are never bought. The pot stays where it is. It is never re-routed to another channel, never swept into anything, never quietly zeroed.The pot, visible on the site, with its balance showing.
The pot is smaller than the smallest packIt waits. There is no partial send, because Kick does not sell partial packs. The pot climbs until it covers a rung.The pot balance, and the first rung once Kick's prices are published.
The streamer turns gifts off, or bans the sending accountPublished. The pot is frozen, still visible, and the site says why it has not become an alert.A line under that channel's pot naming the reason.
The human refuses to sendThe channel was live, the pack was covered, and no alert fired. The gap does not close by itself.A balance that never became an alert, publicly.
The human sends without being toldThe arithmetic stops adding up. The discrepancy is the signal.The sent total exceeds the fee total, publicly.

The operator, plainly

A role that cannot decide can still refuse. The operator can refuse to send: then the pot shows a balance, the channel was live, the pack was covered, and no alert fired. The operator can send without being told: then the total of Kicks sent exceeds the total of fees collected, and the arithmetic on the site stops adding up.

The operator cannot change which channel a coin pays, cannot change the fee, cannot make the alert say a different number, and cannot hide a send, because every send is an alert on someone else's stream.

Note 3If the trigger lived on this site the whole mechanism would be theatre. It lives on the streamer's own stream, where it cannot be forged from here.

What is not verifiable today

ThingState
mintThere is no mint address. When one exists it will appear on the site and in the pinned post at the same moment. Anyone posting one before that is scamming you.
potNo pot has ever bought a Kick. Where a number would go there is a dash, and a dash is not a zero.
ledgerThere is no ledger of sends, so there is no ledger on the site.
ladderThe rung sizes are not published. They will be taken from Kick's own prices at the moment the first pack is bought.
shareKick's share of every gift is not published. It will appear when confirmed from Kick's own terms, with a link.
consentNo streamer has consented to any of this. Coins are launched on channels, not with them.

What is checkable right now is the rule, written down, and this page.
